# House voice — Grounded Editorial Minimalist

The operator's tone of voice for all published copy on every JHD project: sites, apps,
case studies, App Store listings, microcopy, posts. A project's voice guide adds only
project specifics on top of this and never restates it. Operator-facing chat and agent
output styles are not published copy and are out of scope.

## The voice
Direct, plain-spoken, unpretentious, deeply practical. It reads like a high-end independent
design portfolio, not a design agency: no high-brow academic fluff, only clear, honest utility.

## Anti-jargon filter
Strip qualifier padding, agency buzzwords and empty corporate modifiers.

- NEVER use: comprehensive, holistic, robust, seamless, overarching, strategic, integrated, synergy, hyper-realistic, best-in-class, premium, frictionless, distinctive.
- No introductory clauses, meta-commentary or fluff such as "This means that..." or "Our focus is to ensure...". Go straight to the literal point.
- Honest feeling words are not padding. Considered, quiet, crafted and exceptional stay available where they are true (see Warmth over mechanics).

## Sentence structure and grammar
1. **Action-first.** In lists and descriptions, open directly with a strong present-participle verb: "Designing," "Structuring," "Shaping," "Building."
2. **Radical economy.** Say what the thing is without padding. One fluid, authoritative fragment instead of several sentences explaining why it matters. Economy cuts filler, not warmth: a belief that needs a longer sentence gets one.
3. **Conversational but serious.** A human talking directly to another human. No academic tech language; crisp, sharp professionalism. Say it the way you'd explain it to someone across the table: plain words, one concrete example, and no string of three or more abstract nouns ("alignment frameworks for scalable brand ecosystems").
4. **Business-aware.** Ground product stories in user needs, stakeholder requirements and business goals together.
5. **British English.** Colour, organising, modelling.

Action-first openings stay for list and glossary definitions. Long-form copy (case studies) reads as normal sentences.

## Contrast
- BAD (agency fluff): "Crafting comprehensive and holistic brand identity systems across an integrated blueprint to ensure a best-in-class, seamless user experience."
- GOOD (house style): "Building flexible typographic systems, colour palettes, and core visual assets that translate natively between physical spaces and digital interfaces."

## Warmth over mechanics
Operator ruling 2026-10-27 (DISC-210), on copy that read "a bit mechanical" and not nice to read ("100000 times better" for the result). It applies to every page and case study. The point is copy that sounds like a person who cares, so these rules loosen; none adds a prohibition beyond the banned list above. Rules 6 and 7 were added 2026-10-28.

1. **Open with what the person loves and makes**, not their title.
2. **Let sentences run and join, and vary their length.** No lists of three verbs.
3. **Allow a few honest feeling words** (considered, quiet, crafted, exceptional) where they are true.
4. **One idea per paragraph**, stated as a belief rather than explained.
5. **Drop workplace words:** align, iterate, steer, production, deliver.
6. **Don't reuse one sentence shape across a set.** Vary how each line opens and moves.
7. **Say what it's for,** not just what it is.

Framing is general copywriting guidance, not a rule: where a page shows several items together, one line that says what ties them together helps. It is never a required intro, and a page that shows one item at a time (the Glossary, one term each) gets none.

Tone target, approved by the operator (a reference for the sound, not final copy). Before is Profile and Approach draft 3:

- BEFORE (Profile): "I'm a design lead and creative technologist, working across product, web, identity and visual design. I care about how a thing looks, and just as much about whether it works for the people using it and the business behind it. I set the direction, keep the team aligned on it and steer the work to production without losing what made it good."
- AFTER (Profile): "My work is quiet and considered, built on strong typography, clear structure and images that let a brand speak for itself. I love the whole journey, from shaping an idea to seeing it live, and I care as much about how something works as how it looks."
- BEFORE (Approach): "I start with what the business needs and what people need from it, then let taste lead. My taste leans towards restraint, shaped by modernism and by people like Dieter Rams and Steve Jobs: fewer things, each one done properly, and nothing there without a reason. On screen that means a quiet interface, clear type and images that carry the brand. Every brand should feel like itself, never like a template. I share work early, in whatever form reads best, so the whole team can understand it, align and iterate."
- AFTER (Approach): "I start every project by understanding what the business needs and what people need from it, then let taste lead the way. I'm drawn to restraint, the kind you find in modernism and in Dieter Rams's work: fewer things, each one done well. Every brand should feel like itself, never like a template."

## Register reference
Gil Huybrecht's profile page (gilhuybrecht.com/profile) and his overlay for disciplines are the reference for tone: talked through like a conversation, with one concrete example in place of a stack of abstract nouns.

- GOOD: "Laying out information on different pages and fine tuning the funnels tailored to the project's business goals"
- GOOD: "Web design in Figma for desktop and mobile screens. Projects can be done from existing brand guidelines or we define art direction together and work from scratch."
- GOOD: "Specifically doing animations for the website. This includes everything from micro interactions like button hovers to big animations like a page transition, page load animation and so on."
