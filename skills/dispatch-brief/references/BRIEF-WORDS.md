# The brief carries the operator's words, never the parent's guesses

Operator ruling 2026-09-18, `brief-carries-operator-words`: the parent fills the gap
between an operator note and a buildable brief with its own invented specifics —
numbers, mechanisms, statistics — and presents them as the contract. Doers build them
faithfully; the operator sees something they never asked for.

## The rule, in full

1. **The operator's words and images are the contract, verbatim.** The brief quotes
   them and points at the filed images. Parent additions are limited to: paths, the
   locks already agreed (with their letters), gates, ports, the cadence.
2. **No invented numbers.** A value the brief needs is either sourced (a lock, a
   reference measurement, the drawing), derived by the doer from the reference and
   reported, or exposed as a knob for the operator to pick. A number the parent cannot
   source becomes a question in the brief, never a target.
3. **Outcomes and their measurement, never mechanisms.** The brief states what must be
   true and how it is measured at the operator's framing. How to build it is the
   doer's; a mechanism the parent wants considered is offered as "one option, not
   required".
4. **Read back before dispatch on anything visual.** Any brief that interprets a look
   note is played back to the operator in plain words first (`grilling`); the confirmed
   read-back is quoted in the brief. No dispatch on an un-confirmed interpretation.
5. **Short and single.** Under ~150 words plus the lock table; one change per lane;
   rulings never stack into one brief — each new ruling is a new brief on a fresh agent.
6. **The parent's own additions are marked.** Anything in the brief that is not the
   operator's words or a sourced value is prefixed "parent:" so the doer and the
   reviewer can see whose it is.

## Pre-dispatch checklist

```
[ ] Every number sourced or knobbed
[ ] No mechanism prescribed
[ ] Parent additions marked "parent:"
```
